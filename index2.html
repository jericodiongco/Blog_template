I'm a second-year Information Technology student at the University of Cabuyao, where I spend most of my time somewhere between a lecture on data structures and a browser tab full of CSS documentation. I got interested in web development after building a class project that was, honestly, held together with duct tape — but it worked, and that feeling was enough to keep me going.

My educational background started with a general curiosity about how computers actually work, which eventually narrowed down into a specific interest in how websites are built and styled. Through my coursework in web technologies, programming logic, and database fundamentals, I've learned that good software is less about memorizing syntax and more about learning to break a big, messy problem into small, solvable pieces.

My career goal is to grow into a front-end developer who can take a design idea and turn it into a fast, accessible, and genuinely pleasant interface. In the nearer term, I'm focused on getting comfortable with responsive layouts, clean semantic markup, and the kind of small interaction details that make a webpage feel considered rather than default.
